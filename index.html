SN1 Reaction

Definition

SN1 reactions proceed via a carbocation intermediate. Rate depends only on substrate concentration.

Mechanism Steps

  • Step 1: Leaving group departs forming carbocation
  • Step 2: Nucleophile attacks planar carbocation
  • Step 3: Product forms (deprotonation if needed)

Carbocation rearrangements may occur for stability.

Factors Affecting SN1

  • Substrate: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
  • Leaving Group: Better leaving groups speed up the reaction
  • Solvent: Polar protic stabilizes ions
  • Carbocation stability: Allylic/benzylic favored

Stereochemistry

Planar carbocation allows attack from both sides → racemic mixture.

Common Mistakes & Tips

  • Strong nucleophiles do not affect SN1 rate
  • Check for carbocation rearrangements
  • Consider solvent effects

Summary Questions

  • What is the rate-determining step?
  • Why tertiary carbons are favored?
  • Why racemization occurs?

SN2 Reaction

Definition

SN2 reactions proceed in a single concerted step via backside attack; rate depends on both substrate and nucleophile.

Mechanism Steps

  • Step 1: Nucleophile attacks electrophilic carbon from backside
  • Step 2: C–Nu bond forms while C–X breaks
  • Step 3: Product forms with inversion

Factors Affecting SN2

  • Steric hindrance: Methyl > Primary > Secondary
  • Strong nucleophiles favored
  • Good leaving groups required
  • Polar aprotic solvents accelerate reaction

Stereochemistry

Backside attack leads to Walden inversion (100% inversion of configuration).

Common Mistakes & Tips

  • Attempting SN2 on tertiary substrates
  • Ignoring inversion of configuration
  • Using polar protic solvents reduces rate

Summary Questions

  • Why must SN2 attack occur from backside?
  • Why polar aprotic solvents favor SN2?
  • Why are rearrangements impossible in SN2?

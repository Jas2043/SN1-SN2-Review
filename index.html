SN1 Reaction

Definition

SN1 (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular) reactions proceed through a carbocation intermediate. The rate-determining step involves only the substrate.

Mechanism Steps

  • Step 1 (Slow, RDS): Leaving group departs to form a carbocation.
  • Step 2 (Fast): Nucleophile attacks the planar carbocation.
  • Step 3 (If needed): Deprotonation yields the final product.

Carbocation rearrangements may occur to form a more stable intermediate.

Factors Affecting SN1

  • Substrate: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
  • Leaving Group: Better leaving groups increase rate
  • Solvent: Polar protic solvents stabilize ions
  • Carbocation Stability: Allylic and benzylic favored

Stereochemistry

The planar carbocation allows attack from both sides, producing a racemic mixture.

Common Mistakes & Tips

  • Assuming strong nucleophiles speed up SN1 (they do not)
  • Forgetting possible carbocation rearrangements
  • Ignoring solvent effects

Summary Questions

  • What is the rate-determining step of SN1?
  • Why are tertiary carbons favored?
  • Why does SN1 lead to racemization?

SN2 Reaction

Definition

SN2 (Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular) reactions occur in a single, concerted step involving both substrate and nucleophile.

Mechanism Steps

  • Nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon from the backside.
  • C–Nu bond forms as the C–X bond breaks simultaneously.
  • Single transition state, no intermediate.

Factors Affecting SN2

  • Steric Hindrance: Methyl > Primary > Secondary
  • Nucleophile Strength: Strong nucleophiles favored
  • Leaving Group: Better leaving groups increase rate
  • Solvent: Polar aprotic solvents enhance SN2

Stereochemistry

Backside attack results in Walden inversion, giving 100% inversion of configuration.

Common Mistakes & Tips

  • Trying SN2 on tertiary substrates
  • Forgetting stereochemical inversion
  • Using polar protic solvents

Summary Questions

  • Why must SN2 occur via backside attack?
  • Why do polar aprotic solvents favor SN2?
  • Why does SN2 not allow rearrangements?
